# Putting Costing Calculator on the App Store

Everything here needs your Apple ID, your Mac and App Store Connect. None of
it can be done from a Claude session — this is the checklist, not the work.

## Before anything else

**The app has never been compiled.** Every change on this branch was written
and reasoned about but never put through Xcode. Build it, run it on your
phone, use it for a week. Submitting something that has not been built is
putting the cart a long way before the horse.

## What it costs and what it needs

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Apple Developer Program | USD 99 a year, developer.apple.com/programs |
| A Mac with Xcode | Already have it |
| Time for review | Usually 24–48 hours, sometimes longer for a first app |

## Things in this project that must change

**The bundle identifier is a placeholder.** It ships as
`www.abcabc.com.Costing-Calculator`, which is not yours and is not valid
reverse-DNS. Pick something you own — `com.yourcompany.costingcalculator` —
set it in Signing & Capabilities, and register the same string in App Store
Connect. It cannot be changed after the first submission.

**Version numbers.** `MARKETING_VERSION` is 1.0 and
`CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION` is 1. Fine for a first submission. Every later
upload needs the build number raised even if the version stays put.

Already handled in the project:

- App icon at 1024×1024, in the asset catalog
- Launch screen, generated by the build settings
- `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` set to false in `Info.plist` — the app has
  no networking and no encryption of its own, so the export compliance
  question is answered once rather than at every upload
- `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` declaring no tracking, no data collected and none
  of Apple's required-reason APIs. All three are true: costings and photos
  never leave the app's own container

## In App Store Connect

1. **My Apps → +** → New App. Platform iOS, pick the bundle ID you
   registered, choose an SKU (any private string), and a name.
2. **App Privacy** → **Data Not Collected**. The app has no networking; that
   answer is accurate and the privacy manifest already says the same.
3. **Age rating** questionnaire — all no, which gives 4+.
4. **Screenshots.** Required for 6.9" and 6.5" iPhones. They must come from
   the real app, on a device or simulator — Apple requires screenshots show
   the actual app, so the web preview from this session cannot be used.
   ⌘S in the simulator saves one to the desktop.
5. **Description, keywords, support URL.** A support URL is mandatory and
   must resolve; a plain page with an email address is enough.

## Archiving and uploading

1. Select **Any iOS Device** as the destination — not a simulator.
2. **Product → Archive**.
3. In the Organizer, **Distribute App → App Store Connect → Upload**.
4. Wait for processing, then pick the build in App Store Connect and submit.

## Two things worth deciding first

**This may be the wrong shop.** Guideline 4.2 lets Apple reject apps of
narrow utility, and a costing sheet for one company's own moulding work is
exactly the shape of app that draws that question. It is a real risk, not a
certainty.

**A Custom App may fit better.** Apple Business Manager can distribute an
app privately to your own staff: no public listing, no review of whether it
is broadly useful, and no need to explain injection cycle times to a
reviewer. Same developer account. If the people using this are your own
team, that is the shorter road — worth looking at before paying for a
public listing.

TestFlight is the third option and needs no review at all for internal
testers, up to 100 people. It is the fastest way to get this onto other
people's phones.
